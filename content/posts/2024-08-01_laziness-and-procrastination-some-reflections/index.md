I have two habits that are arguably my worst: laziness and procrastination.

<!--more-->

# Awareness

> You are below the average standard, or worse, at the very lowest standard.

***What does the average standard include here?***
- Taking good care of yourself:
    - Health: no serious or unusual illnesses, good physical fitness
    - Appearance: normal BMI, not too fat or too thin, daily personal hygiene (skin, hair, teeth)
- Independence:
    - Job: a contract, an annual salary, insurance
    - Assets: enough to live on at your income level, with a backup that can cover 6 months to 1 year
    - Discipline: having your own self-discipline; it may not be 100% strict, but it still builds good habits
    - Emotional control: no longer overly candid; you still have emotions, but you can hide them to suit social interactions

> You realize you haven't done anything at all. You're stuck in a loop of "hundreds of goals - half-hearted action - forgetting", which then gradually shrinks until you only set goals and think about them in your head. Only when things have become too serious or almost beyond saving do you finally feel regret, so what's the point of regretting 😂

# Causes

> *Self-awareness matters most, but the environment is what nurtures a person.*

- Family:
    - No proper guidance (every family has a different background)
    - An overprotective and rigid environment: not being allowed to follow your own wishes even when they aren't bad, living a smooth, sheltered life and depending on others
- School and work environment:
    - Not many people around who are skilled enough or good enough: this also depends on luck
- Myself: I know I'm in a bad state, but I'm not brave or strong enough to overcome it. I'm aware of it, but probably not aware enough, and not scared enough of how serious the problem is

# Lessons and fixes

- Family: you should separate yourself from it
- Environment: first, you have to dare to take action and be good enough yourself:
    - Going to the gym: you meet healthy, fit people
    - Social media/lifestyle of beautiful people and actors: it makes you want to look better
    - Summits, hackathons, communities: you are surrounded by capable people with a drive to keep improving
- Emotions and communication:
    - Books: you learn from real-life stories. Even if you don't get enough practice in real situations, books give you enough knowledge and advance awareness of the cases life throws at you, so you know the principles and can adapt in many situations
    - Videos (TED Talks, podcasts): the speakers are people who share, communicate and talk a lot, so observe them and shadow them
- Motivation:
    - What matters is keeping it going, not motivation itself, but you still need a good kick-start
    - Keeping it going means adding more motivation every day. I think motivation is fuel, not a single step on the gas pedal, so keep pumping it in every day
